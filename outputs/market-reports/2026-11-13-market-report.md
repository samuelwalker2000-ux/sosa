### 2. MY TAKE

This is a classic late-cycle risk flush, not a one-day wobble. BTC cracking $70K on sustained ETF outflows — $1.44B over five sessions with IBIT driving 90% of Nov 11 withdrawals — tells you the institutional bid that powered the rally through 2025 is on the sidelines. The catalyst is obvious: a dollar at 19-month highs, 10Y yields near 5.1%, and Fed rate hike expectations now above 90% for December. That macro configuration is structurally negative for all risk assets, and crypto is the highest-beta expression of that pain.

The positioning call is defensive and patient. BTC at $68,830 is ~45% off its 2025 all-time high — this isn't a dip to buy yet. Wait for PPI to clear (today), watch how the dollar reacts to the number, and don't confuse a dead-cat bounce from oversold conditions with a reversal. If the PPI is hot and the DXY pushes above its 19-month high, the next support cluster for BTC is in the $63K–$65K range. Cash and short duration are the right posture until the Fed signals a credible pause.

### 9. FITNESS

**Today's insight:** A 2026 study in the *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research* compared three 12-week training models for masters trail runners (aged 35–55): circuit weight training alone, uphill interval running alone, and a combined protocol. The combined group showed the broadest physiological adaptations — circuit training improved running economy on flat terrain while uphill intervals improved it on grades. The practical takeaway: if you're a runner over 35, adding 2–3 days per week of lower-body circuit work alongside your interval sessions is more effective than doing either in isolation. Focus on compound lifts (squats, Romanian deadlifts, calf raises) and hill repeats for maximum transfer.

### 10. MEP ENGINEERING

**Today's technical item:** ASHRAE is overhauling Standard 90.1 (Energy Standard for Sites and Buildings) by separating energy efficiency requirements from carbon emissions rules into two distinct standards. The move — reported by ACHR News — reflects the growing complexity of decarbonisation codes and allows jurisdictions to adopt energy provisions independently of carbon limits, addressing conflicts that arise when low-carbon fuels (e.g., heat pumps, electrification) don't neatly align with traditional energy-use intensity metrics. For Ontario-based MEP engineers, this restructuring will influence how future OBC energy compliance paths are drafted, particularly for Part 3 buildings targeting net-zero or low-carbon performance. Additionally, two addenda to ASHRAE 205-2023 (performance data for HVAC&R equipment) are in public review until November 22, 2026 — worth reviewing if you work with equipment selection software or digital twin applications.

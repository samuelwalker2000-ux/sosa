### 2. MY TAKE

The soft CPI print is the real story today — not the BTC rally. A 3.5% headline print against a 3.8% forecast is material. Rate hike odds got cut in half overnight. That alone is enough to justify the crypto bounce and the mild equity bid. But don't confuse a relief rally with a trend reversal: the 10Y is still at 4.62%, DXY is sticky around 100, and oil is spiking on geopolitical noise. The macro ceiling hasn't moved — it's just been raised slightly for one session.

The trade here is patient offense on BTC. Whales absorbed 270,000 BTC (~$16.7B) over the past two weeks while ETFs bled. Exchange reserves are at 7-year lows. That kind of accumulation at these levels, combined with ETF flows turning positive again (IBIT +$139M yesterday), is the setup for a sustained move — not a day trade. The risk is a July oil shock or a Fed re-hawking if subsequent data surprises. Hold core BTC, trim levered alts, and wait for the FOMC on July 29-30 before adding conviction.

### 9. FITNESS

**Today's insight:** Eccentric strength training — the slow, controlled lengthening of a muscle under load — is one of the most evidence-backed tools for runner longevity and performance. A 2024-2025 randomised controlled trial confirmed that strength training improves *running economy durability*: athletes trained with lifting maintain their efficiency under fatigue, not just in fresh conditions. The practical application is simple — add 2x weekly sessions of slow-tempo exercises (Nordic curls, single-leg Romanian deadlifts, calf lowering) at weights that are challenging but don't compromise your next run. Tendon tissue remodels over 6-12 weeks of this stimulus, reducing injury risk and improving late-race output.

### 10. MEP ENGINEERING

**Today's technical item:** The 2026 commercial HVAC codes (driven by IECC 2024 and ASHRAE 90.1-2022) are now requiring A2L mildly-flammable refrigerants — primarily R-32 and R-454B — as the default for new commercial installations following the January 2025 R-410A manufacturing ban. This is not optional: all new VRF, chiller, and split system equipment specified from 2025 forward must use A2L-compatible refrigerants. For MEP engineers, the practical implications are immediate: room occupancy limits, ventilation requirements for refrigerant concentration, and equipment room sizing all change under ASHRAE 15-2022 provisions for A2L systems. Ensure that equipment submittals are reviewed against the updated safety classification and that field technicians are trained on A2L handling procedures before commissioning.

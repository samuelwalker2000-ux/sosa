### 2. MY TAKE

BTC is in a confirmed short-term downtrend — down ~10% in a week, sliding through the $70K level that held as support in October, and now threatening the August lows around $65K–66K. The catalyst isn't a black-swan event; it's sustained institutional selling via ETF redemptions and a rising real yield environment that makes holding zero-yield assets structurally expensive. IBIT outflows of ~$1.5B in two weeks are not noise.

The right stance here is **defensive and patient**. Do not buy the dip until one of three things happens: (1) ETF flows stabilize and reverse, (2) the 10Y yield peaks and reverses, or (3) BTC holds the $65K zone on high volume and closes a weekly candle above $70K. Cash and short-duration bonds are earning real yield. There is no urgency to be long crypto or high-multiple equities right now — wait for confirmation.

### 9. FITNESS

**Today's insight:** Concurrent strength training — lifting heavy 2–3x per week alongside your running program — has been shown to improve running economy by 2–8%, meaning you burn less energy at a given pace. The mechanism is better neuromuscular efficiency and improved force production, not muscle mass gain. Prioritize compound lower-body lifts (squat, Romanian deadlift, single-leg work) and plyometrics over high-rep pump work; the neural adaptation is what transfers to running performance.

### 10. MEP ENGINEERING

**Today's technical item:** ASHRAE is overhauling Standard 90.1 (Energy Standard for Buildings) to formally separate energy efficiency requirements from operational carbon metrics — a structural change that will affect how MEP engineers design and document building systems going forward. Previously, energy and carbon were bundled; the new framework will likely require engineers to report compliance paths independently for each metric. A first public review for new HVAC-related MEP product category rules closes December 1, 2026 — practitioners should review and submit comments before that deadline.

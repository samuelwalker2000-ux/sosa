### 2. MY TAKE

This is a relief bounce sitting inside a downtrend, not a reversal. BTC reclaiming $60K after touching a 20-month low near $58K matters less than the fact that sentiment hasn't moved — Extreme Fear at 17 for a second straight day means the crowd doesn't believe the bounce yet, and the most recent ETF flow data we can confirm (ARKB +$64M on June 22) is already five trading days stale, which tells you the marginal institutional buyer hasn't visibly stepped back in. Meanwhile equities are quietly doing the more interesting thing: the Nasdaq-100's fifth consecutive losing session, driven by AI-capex cost fears (Apple and Microsoft both hiking hardware prices, a reported OpenAI IPO delay) while the S&P stays flat, is a genuine rotation out of high-multiple AI/tech and into defensives — not a single-day wobble.

My stance is patient, not defensive-to-the-point-of-cash, and not aggressive either. Micron's blowout earnings (+15.7%) is real evidence AI-linked hardware demand hasn't cracked, so this isn't 2022-style capitulation — it's a market re-pricing who pays for AI capex versus who's still proving it can monetize. I want BTC to hold above $58K and see ETF flows actually confirmed positive again before treating $60K as a floor rather than a bounce; on the equity side, I'd rather wait for the Nasdaq-100 selloff to find a level than buy the dip into a fifth red candle. Jobs report on July 2 is the next real test for both.

### 9. FITNESS

**Today's insight:** A randomized controlled trial in well-trained male runners found that adding strength training improved running economy "durability" — i.e., runners maintained efficient form for longer under fatigue — and boosted high-intensity performance even when fatigued, not just at rest. The practical takeaway: 2x/week strength work pays off most when your run gets hard late, not just on your easy-pace splits.

### 10. MEP ENGINEERING

**Today's technical item:** States have a hard certification deadline of early 2026 to formally adopt ASHRAE 90.1-2022 (Alabama, Indiana, New Jersey, Oregon, West Virginia, and DC are already there). The big practical change in this edition is Section 8's mandatory Energy Management Control System requirement for buildings over 25,000 sq ft: continuous 15-minute-interval monitoring by load category (HVAC, interior/exterior lighting, plug, process) with 36-month data retention — meaning BAS specs on any mid-size+ project now need to budget for that monitoring/retention infrastructure up front, not as an add-on.

### 9. FITNESS

**Today's insight:** Heavy strength training directly improves running economy — and you don't need to gain weight to get the benefit. A well-cited study (Hoff et al.) had well-trained endurance runners add heavy squats 3× per week for 8 weeks; running economy improved significantly versus the endurance-only control group, with no change in VO₂ max or body mass. The mechanism is improved neuromuscular efficiency: heavier loads recruit more motor units, teaching your legs to apply force more effectively at sub-maximal running intensities. Practical takeaway: if you run and do zero strength work, even 2 heavy lower-body sessions per week (squats, Romanian deadlifts) for 2 months will make you a meaningfully faster and more efficient runner.

### 10. MEP ENGINEERING

**Today's technical item:** A2L refrigerant piping shaft requirements under the 2024 International Mechanical Code are a live compliance issue for Canadian MEP engineers. Under the 2024 IMC, refrigerant lines for A2L refrigerant systems (R-32, R-454B, R-452B — now dominating commercial HVAC) that penetrate two or more floor/ceiling assemblies must be located in a ventilated, fire-rated shaft per ASHRAE 15. This adds shaft coordination and fire-stopping requirements that were absent for legacy HFC systems. Note: The ICC's 2027 code development cycle has approved changes to the IMC that would eliminate this shaft requirement as ASHRAE 15 has since been updated, but Canadian jurisdictions won't adopt 2027 IMC for several years — design teams working under current OBC/NBC referencing the 2024 IMC need to treat the shaft requirement as active. Coordinate early with structural and architecture on any multi-storey A2L system installation.

### 2. MY TAKE

The setup right now is defensively biased — be patient. The 10Y yield cracking 5.35% is a genuine regime signal. Every time yields spike to multi-decade highs the narrative shifts from "higher for longer is priced in" to "something is going to break." Equity indexes making records on Oct 6 then reversing on Oct 7 is a classic distribution pattern: bulls exhaust themselves at new highs while real money quietly reduces duration exposure. The BTC ETF outflow of $484.9M on a single day after a "best weekly close since January" is a textbook buy-the-rumour-sell-the-news move by institutional participants.

Stay in cash or short-duration instruments on a meaningful portion of the portfolio. If you're in crypto, BTC at $82K holding the $82,317 intraday low is the critical support — breach it decisively and $78-79K is the next stop with nothing technical in between. Don't get cute catching altcoin bounces in this environment; the alt market is in risk-off mode and BTC dominance pushing toward 59% confirms alts will lag on any BTC drawdown. Wait for the CPI print on Oct 14 before adding risk — a softer number could flip the yield narrative fast, but you don't need to be positioned ahead of it.

### 10. MEP ENGINEERING

**Today's technical item:** ASHRAE has formally opened public review of an addendum to Standard 205-2023 (Representation of Performance Data for HVAC&R Equipment), aligning it with Standard 232-2024 on building data schemas — a step toward machine-readable, interoperable equipment performance data across BAS/EMCS platforms. For engineers specifying or commissioning complex systems, this matters: once Standard 205 adoption reaches manufacturers, you'll be able to pull certified, standardised performance curves directly into simulation tools rather than relying on manufacturer-specific exports. ASHRAE's 2026–27 agenda under incoming president Sarah Maston also prioritises retrofitting for resilience, meaning upcoming standards guidance will increasingly address adaptive envelope upgrades and heat pump integration into existing building systems.

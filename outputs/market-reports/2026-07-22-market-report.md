### 9. FITNESS

**Today's insight:** The American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) released its first updated resistance training guidelines in 17 years in 2026, reinforcing that moving from zero resistance training to any resistance training produces the most meaningful gains — consistency trumps program complexity. For runners specifically, the new guidance emphasises eccentric loading (slow, controlled lengthening under load) to remodel tendon tissue, restore load tolerance, and reduce injury risk — a direct response to years of research showing running economy improvements of 4% and VO2max gains of 4.6% from strength-concurrent training. The takeaway: two strength sessions per week with a focus on single-leg eccentric work (Bulgarian split squats, single-leg Romanian deadlifts) will make your running feel easier and your late-race pace more sustainable.

### 10. MEP ENGINEERING

**Today's technical item:** ASHRAE Standard 90.1 (2025 edition, now in force) introduced a requirement for healthcare facilities to implement unoccupied setbacks in certain clinical spaces — reducing ventilation rates and adjusting temperature setpoints when specific areas are unoccupied. For MEP engineers, this changes the design calculus on VAV systems in clinic corridors, procedure rooms, and diagnostic imaging: systems must now be designed with BAS-integrated occupancy sensing capable of meeting both the occupied ventilation minimums and demonstrably achieving the setback savings for code compliance. Additionally, ASHRAE's liquid cooling standards for data centre applications remain unfinalized as of mid-2026 — engineers specifying immersion or direct liquid cooling for AI-infrastructure projects are still working from manufacturer specs, meaning design risk sits with the engineer of record until formal standards land (expected late 2026).

### 2. MY TAKE

The Fed just broke a three-year silence by hiking 25bps, and the playbook from here is straightforward: the dollar will bid, bonds will reprice higher in yield, and any equity sector without pricing power gets compressed. Chair Warsh's language was deliberate — "inflation remains elevated" with another hike possibly in October. This is not a one-and-done. Rotate into commodities (oil, gold), energy names, and consumer defensives. Underweight rate-sensitive growth tech and long-duration bonds. The crypto pop today is a short-term relief trade, not a signal to overload — BTC holding $80K is constructive but the macro headwind is real if yields push back above 5%.

Patient and selective is the posture. The rotation into industrials and energy is already underway and still has legs — Caterpillar and Exxon are printing multi-year highs for a reason. Don't chase crypto alts on this bounce; the liquidity math doesn't favour speculative assets when real yields are rising. Hold core BTC positions, trim high-beta alts, accumulate GLD on any pullback, and wait for equity valuations to compress further before adding to QQQ.

### 9. FITNESS

**Today's insight:** A 2025 randomised controlled trial published in a peer-reviewed journal confirmed that strength training improves **running economy durability** — meaning trained runners maintain their efficiency under fatigue rather than just in fresh conditions. The practical implication: add 2x/week heavy compound lifts (trap bar deadlifts, Bulgarian split squats) at 70–85% 1RM during base-building phases, not just during taper. This is the most underutilised performance lever for recreational runners chasing sub-4-hour marathons or faster 5K times — the cardiovascular system often isn't the limiter; mechanical efficiency collapse late in the race is. *(Source: ResearchGate / Strength Training Improves Running Economy Durability, 2025)*

### 10. MEP ENGINEERING

**Today's technical item:** The **2026 NEC (National Electrical Code)** introduces a significant shift in load calculation methodology — Article 130 now emphasises **Energy Management Systems (EMS)** as the basis for service sizing, allowing engineers to calculate maximum demand based on actual monitored loads rather than worst-case connected load. This prevents chronic over-sizing of electrical services in commercial and institutional buildings. Simultaneously, arc-flash safety labelling is now mandatory for *all* service equipment regardless of amperage — not just panels above the previous threshold — so MEP engineers must factor NFPA 70E-compliant arc-flash studies into every electrical design deliverable. Failure to include labelling requirements in project specifications is now a code non-compliance, not just a best practice gap. *(Source: ASHRAE Standards / NEC 2026 updates)*

### 2. MY TAKE

This is a genuine regime change, not a dip-buying opportunity — at least not yet. Nvidia's situation is a convergence of three separate shocks: Jensen Huang's resignation, an SEC accounting probe that could be existential for disclosures, and a broader market that had priced AI chipmakers for perfection. When the central pillar of a bull narrative breaks down — and NVDA losing $2.7T in a week is exactly that — the reflexive response is to sell everything correlated, which is essentially the entire Nasdaq. The S&P is in correction, the Nasdaq has gone negative for 2026, and BTC spot ETFs are hemorrhaging $582M per session. These are not soft signals.

The only credible near-term path to stabilization is for the SEC probe to show it's narrow and quickly resolved, or for a major Fed pivot signal — neither is imminent. For now, the positioning is defensive: raise cash on bounces, avoid adding tech exposure, and watch the Nov 25 PCE print and Dec FOMC for inflection points. If BTC holds $55K and ETF outflows slow, that's the first signal the crypto leg is finding a floor. Until then, patience is capital preservation.

### 9. FITNESS

**Today's insight:** Heavy and explosive lower-body strength training 2–3 times per week measurably improves running economy and speed for endurance runners. A 2018 systematic review in *Sports Medicine* found that combining heavy resistance, explosive resistance, and plyometric work produces the strongest adaptations — focus on power output (reps 2–8, full recovery between sets), not just stability or endurance reps. For most runners who aren't already highly trained, this is a higher-ROI training block than adding more easy mileage.

### 10. MEP ENGINEERING

**Today's technical item:** ASHRAE is structurally separating energy efficiency requirements and operational carbon reduction requirements in Standard 90.1. In a July 2026 announcement, ASHRAE confirmed that the carbon-focused addenda (originally Addenda M and N to 90.1-2025) will be published as a standalone companion document rather than embedded in the core 90.1 standard. The practical implication for MEP engineers: jurisdictions can now reference 90.1 for pure energy efficiency compliance and opt into the carbon standard separately — meaning your project's compliance path will increasingly depend on which documents your AHJ has adopted. Watch for OBC and state energy code cycles to begin citing (or explicitly declining to adopt) the carbon companion standard, which will drive different MEP design requirements in energy-code-progressive vs conservative jurisdictions.

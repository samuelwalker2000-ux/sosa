### 9. FITNESS

**Today's insight:** A meta-analysis of strength training for middle- and long-distance runners found a consistent 2–8% improvement in running economy across studies, with the largest gains coming from heavy compound lifts (squats, deadlifts, plyometrics) performed 2× per week as a supplement to normal run training. The mechanism is improved neuromuscular efficiency — you recruit more fast-twitch fibres before fatiguing, which matters most in the final kilometres of a race or a long run. The practical takeaway: even 20–30 minutes of heavy lower-body strength work twice a week has meaningful carry-over to endurance performance; you don't need a bodybuilder programme, just consistent load.

### 10. MEP ENGINEERING

**Today's technical item:** As of January 1, 2026, the EPA's HFC phasedown regulations prohibit the installation of new HVAC/R equipment using refrigerants with a Global Warming Potential (GWP) above 700 in most applications — a threshold that eliminates R-410A (GWP ~2,088) from new installations. The industry has rapidly pivoted to A2L refrigerants (R-32, R-454B, R-466A), which are mildly flammable and require updated equipment room ventilation, leak detection, and installer training under revised ASHRAE, NFPA, and mechanical code provisions. For Canadian projects, the Ontario Building Code is tracking similar phasedown language aligned with the Kigali Amendment — MEP designers specifying chillers, RTUs, and split systems in 2026–2027 projects must confirm A2L compliance in equipment submittals and verify that refrigerant rooms meet the new ventilation and detector requirements.

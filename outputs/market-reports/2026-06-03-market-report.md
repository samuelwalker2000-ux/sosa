### 9. FITNESS

**Today's insight:** A 2026 randomised controlled trial (PLoS ONE, PMC12885319) found that **high time-under-tension (TUT) strength training** improves neuromuscular efficiency in runners without causing the level of post-exercise fatigue associated with traditional heavy-load protocols. For distance runners, 3–4 sets of slow-tempo (4-second eccentric, 2-second concentric) squats, single-leg deadlifts, and hip thrusts twice a week appear to improve running economy more effectively than equivalent volume at standard tempo. The mechanism: greater motor unit recruitment during the slow phase, building the tendon stiffness and intra-muscular coordination that reduces the metabolic cost of the running gait.

### 2. MY TAKE

The post-midterm crypto unwind is a textbook "sell the news" — the market priced in a favorable political outcome, ran it to $80k, and institutional holders immediately began distributing. BlackRock's IBIT alone dumped $403M in a single session. That's not retail panic; that's structured profit-taking by the same hands that pushed prices up. Fear & Greed at 25 is historically a buy zone, but you don't catch a falling knife just because sentiment is stretched — wait for ETF flows to turn positive again before treating this as a dip-buying opportunity.

On equities, the Friday bounce is tactical noise. The underlying problem is that the AI trade is showing signs of crowding and disappointing forward guidance — Axon -18% on an earnings miss, Microchip Tech -13%. The VIX at 25 tells you hedging remains expensive. The play here is patient: hold quality, reduce AI concentration, and let the CPI print on Monday do the work. If inflation surprises lower, the December rate cut odds improve and you get a better entry. If it comes in hot with yields pushing through 5%, cut risk. No FOMO, no heroics — patient is the right posture right now.

### 9. FITNESS

**Today's insight:** Strength training improves running economy by 2–8% in endurance runners, according to a July 2026 narrative review in *Quality in Sport*, with benefits spanning muscular strength, hip stability, and lumbopelvic control. The key caveat: several studies that showed gains also reduced running mileage significantly during the intervention, so the economy improvement may partly reflect lower accumulated fatigue rather than pure strength gains. Practical rule — add 2 strength sessions per week on top of your current run volume; don't substitute them for mileage, and watch total load to isolate what's actually moving the needle.

### 10. MEP ENGINEERING

**Today's technical item:** Chemours has launched a range of two-phase cooling fluids (direct-to-chip and immersion variants) targeting high-density data centers — a direct response to AI server rack power densities now routinely exceeding 100 kW/rack, which has pushed traditional air-side HVAC to its practical limits. For MEP engineers, this signals that two-phase liquid cooling is moving from niche to mainstream in mission-critical facility design; ASHRAE TC 9.9 guidance on data center cooling will increasingly reference immersion and direct-to-chip systems alongside traditional CRAC/CRAH. Specifying engineers should start familiarizing themselves with the thermodynamic design requirements and materials compatibility considerations for these systems, as project RFPs are beginning to include them as preferred options.

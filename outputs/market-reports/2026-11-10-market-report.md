### 9. FITNESS

**Today's insight:** A 2025 randomised controlled trial in *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise* found that adding a 10-week strength and plyometric program (2x/week) to trained male runners' schedules preserved running economy later in long runs and significantly extended time-to-exhaustion during high-intensity efforts. The key mechanism: heavy lower-body work (especially calf raises) builds stiffness in the calf-Achilles spring, improving energy return efficiency. Practical application: 2–3 sessions per week of single-leg calf raises, Romanian deadlifts, and box jumps is enough stimulus — prioritise quality over volume, and schedule strength sessions on easy run days to avoid compromising hard sessions.

### 10. MEP ENGINEERING

**Today's technical item:** ASHRAE has updated Standard 90.1 (Energy Standard for Sites and Buildings Except Low-Rise Residential Buildings) to include pump definitions, performance requirements, and efficiency tables for the first time — a significant addition for mechanical engineers specifying hydronic systems on commercial projects. For the first time, pumping systems are explicitly addressed in the energy standard's requirements, closing a gap that previously left pump selection largely unregulated from an energy compliance standpoint. Concurrently, ASHRAE's proposed addendum to Standard 62.1-2025 (Ventilation for Acceptable Indoor Air Quality) is in public review until November 8, 2026 — if you have comments on ventilation rate changes, that window has just closed, but the addendum is worth tracking as it moves toward adoption.
